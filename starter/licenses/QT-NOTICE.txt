You may modify and replace the Qt libraries, and reverse engineer Agent Pet for
debugging your modifications to those libraries. Use a compatible Qt 6 build
with the same architecture and compiler ABI. Stop Agent Pet and its updater,
back up the installation, replace the libraries and plugins, then restart:

Linux portable bundle: replace Qt shared libraries in lib/ and their plugins
in plugins/. For system packages, use your distribution's Qt packages or rebuild
Agent Pet against your modified Qt (see the source archive's README.md).

Windows: replace Qt6*.dll beside agent-pet.exe and the matching plugins in the
platforms/, imageformats/, styles/ and tls/ directories that are present.
Keep the compiler runtime and your replacement Qt build ABI compatible.

macOS: in Agent Pet.app (Finder: Show Package Contents), replace the Qt
frameworks in Contents/Frameworks/ and matching plugins in Contents/PlugIns/.
After modification, re-sign your local copy in Terminal:
  codesign --force --deep --sign - --timestamp=none "/path/to/Agent Pet.app"
Then launch your modified copy normally. Official bundles use ad hoc signing;
no developer signing key is required to re-sign your local copy.

Updates may replace your modified libraries. Keep a separate portable copy
and decline updates if you want to retain them.

Bundled releases include version-specific source download directions in
runtime-licenses/qt/SOURCES.txt (on Linux under share/agent-pet/, on macOS
under Contents/Resources/). Official releases also provide the Qt source
archives as separate assets on the same GitHub release, at no charge:
https://github.com/WindyWin/vpet-agent-pet/releases
System Qt sources, including distribution patches, come from the corresponding
distribution source packages. Publishers of custom bundles must provide the
matching sources and update SOURCES.txt, including any patches and build scripts.
